Liam Pheng    
## Interviewees:
c/LTC Battalion Commander Lionel Carapia

**Question: What have your experiences been like with military communication through apps**

LC: In all the units I’ve been in I’ve seen a lot of different ones being used and it makes it difficult when transferring to different units and adjusting to what system they use.
HS: They have for the most part been straight forward. Using teams and signals are what I prefer and for the most part a lot of units that I’ve seen make use of these systems. 
EH: I’ve used a bunch of different ones but for the most part communication using apps are pretty decent

**Question: What applications have you guys seen in the force (army)**

LC: I’ve used a lot of different ones (applications) like Teams, GroupMe, Text Messaging Group chats, and signal.
HS: The units I’ve been in have mainly utilized teams and signal but I’ve heard people use blueforce tracker and a bunch of other ones
EH: Mainly Teams, Signal and GroupMe, but it is always different everywhere you go

**Question: Have you found that across different levels of the Army there are different communication channels used?**

LC: Yes definitely, when I was at Knox (Ft. Knox, Kentucky) my company was using signal chat while my platoon and squads were using text messaging. 
HS: Yeah, I mainly use Teams chat to communicate with my AS3s (assistant operations officers) but I use signal to communicate with Battalion command
EH: Yeah, a lot of MS4s (senior ROTC Cadets) use Snapchat or text messaging to communicate informally but formally within Battalion I see Signal or teams used

**Question: What are some of the issues you have seen while using apps like Microsoft Teams or Signal?**

LC: One of the things that I always see is that joes (lower enlisted individuals) always are messaging platoon leaders or company commanders directly and aren’t following the proper chain of command protocols. This is a serious issue in communication because it means that military bearing and professionalism aren’t being upheld.
HS: To bounce off of what Carapia just said, I also have the issue where people are texting or messaging me directly rather than talking to their team or squad leaders. A lot of the questions that are being asked are also answered in different channels or are published and they just aren’t seeing it because they're looking in the wrong app.
EH: The biggest issue I'm seeing with it (Microsoft Teams) is that it wasn't built specifically for military use so it lacks some features and abilities that would make pushing out information and sending orders up and down the chain of command way more convenient

**Question: Does the wide variety of used apps cause miscommunication or any issues disseminating information?**

LC: The non uniformity of communication in units is a prevalent issue I've seen throughout all units I've been a part of
HS: Not having a standard app has made pushing out information from the S3 (operations) shop a lot more difficult because we are unaware if lower leadership has given out the correct information because we can't see those channels
EH: Definitely, I am always asked questions that I have already answered in OPORDs or messages sent in Signal. People don’t check the right communication channels or they are using outdated information sent out previously

**Question: Would having a set standardized communication channel or app be beneficial?**

LC: Yes
HS: Yes
EH: Yes

**Question: What features would you like to see in a military based communication app?**

LC: A way to enforce chain of command. Like having lower ranked individuals not be able to communicate or send messages directly to their Battalion Commander and instead only be able to talk to their squad members or squad leader
HS: Exactly what he said. The way to utilize the chain of command and not skip it would definitely be a huge feature that a lot of what we use today don't have. 
EH: I would say a set backbrief feature where squad leaders can put out a question or something and have answers and submissions like an assignment on canvas. Also having a way to have publicly released documents like "On Eagles Wings" (Informative document) or the weekly OPORD available for everyone to see would be cool.

**Question: Would a calendar feature with events be something that interests you in a military accountability app?**

LC: We already have the LRTC (long range training calendar) in the OPORD and on the Sharepoint for those that want to look ahead, but if it is in the same app as the chat it would be convenient
HS: I think that we should enforce the use of the LRTC rather than having the information in the app because it causes people to not read the OPORD. We already have issues with people not showing up and not reading the weekly OPORD so I don't think that this would encourage good behaviour
EH: I disagree with Hunter. Having the LRTC synced with a system like google calendar would be super useful and convenient in one place

**Question: Would an accountability feature be helpful in this hypothetical app?**

LC: It depends what you mean by accountability 

**Elaborated: Like having a way to show who is required to be at a battalion event and then have an attached accountability list to check off the day of**

LC: Definitely, right now the spreadsheets are all over the place and if we could put them in the same place and have the ability to check off as we go would be huge
HS: That would definitely be a huge bonus, being able to have an accountability tracker in the same place
EH: I think that would be very helpful especially because we have a million different trackers and accountability lists all over the sharepoint. If we could consolidate PT accountability trackers and Lab accountability we would be much more organized.
